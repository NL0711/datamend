# Multi-scale band detection: owner's notes mapped against what exists

Status: **PLANNED. Nothing in this file is built or run.** Written 2026-11-19 from the owner's pasted notes and a read of the code on branch `feature/ingest-pipeline`. This is a plan and a gap list, not a result, and it makes no novelty claim (AGENTS.md section 5). ## 1. The owner's notes, condensed

- Data needed per timescale: hourly or diurnal work wants at least 1 to 2 years of hourly data (diurnal and seasonal cycle); synoptic (days) work wants 10 to 30 years of daily data. `not verified` (the owner's rule of thumb).
- Method: decompose the signal first, then detect per band. Bands named: high-frequency (synoptic), intraseasonal (the 30 to 90 day, MJO band), seasonal, interannual, trend. Tools named: wavelets or band-pass filters (for example Lanczos). Compute anomalies inside each band, run detection in each band separately, then recombine the flags to describe the event.
- Eight steps: (1) pick target timescale(s); (2) a reference period long enough to sample that timescale many times; (3) detrend if a climate trend would contaminate the baseline, or model it explicitly; (4) decompose into bands if multi-scale; (5) climatology per band (mean and std, or quantiles); (6) for ML, train an autoencoder or Isolation Forest per band on the training period; (7) validate on held-out recent years and on known events (the owner names the 2003 European heatwave, the 2010 Russian heatwave, the 2021 Pacific North-West heat dome); (8) threshold tails with extreme-value theory (GEV or GPD) instead of Gaussian z-scores.

## 2. What the repo contains (read from the code, 2026-11-19)

Searched `*.py` for wavelet, Lanczos, band-pass, GEV, GPD (`genextreme`, `genpareto`), Isolation Forest, autoencoder, detrend, MJO, intraseasonal, trend, fft, `scipy.signal`, `pywt`.

- **Not present anywhere in code:** wavelets, Lanczos or any band-pass filter, MJO or intraseasonal band, GEV, GPD or any peaks-over-threshold fit, Isolation Forest, any explicit detrending or trend model, any hourly or diurnal-cycle model. The only hits in documents are literature notes: Isolation Forest (temperature and humidity LoRaWAN stations) and a GPD fit used in a surprisal paper (`docs/research/slr-multivariate-weather-qc-architectures-20261117.md`, `docs/research/slr-weather-anomaly-detection-models.md`; `read` there, not re-checked here), and an aggregated wavelet tree clustering paper listed in the second file.
- **Present, partly related:**
  - A **time-scale ladder by aggregation, not by filtering**: `ml-service/qc/resolution.py` builds daily, monthly, seasonal (June to September) and yearly periods from RF25 daily grids (`aggregate`, `period_ids`, `fit_scale_stats` on training years only) and decade means (`yearly_to_decades`). Each scale has its own scorers (`scale_scorers.py`: `gamma_zero`, `loo_median`, `loo_kriging`, `gbm_neighbour`, `mlp_neighbour`), a frozen synthetic benchmark (`period_benchmark.py`), validation-only selection (`scale_select.py`), and fixed or learned cross-scale fusion with a coherent-extreme discount (`crossscale.py`, `crossscale_run.py`, `crossscale_test.py`). Spec: `docs/qc-multires-pipeline-spec.md`. All of it is unit-tested on generated data only; nothing has run on real RF25 for the period scales.
  - **Seasonal cycle handled as climatology, not as a band:** the daily check takes gamma fits on earlier wet days within a 15-day window of the day of year (`backend/app/validation/checks.py`, `_gamma_tail`, `CLIM_WINDOW_DAYS`, `CLIM_MIN_WET = 30`, tail probability `CLIM_TAIL_P = 1e-3`); the period scorers use per-calendar-month and per-period climatology (`GammaZeroScorer`, `anomaly()` = log1p total minus log1p climatology); the STNet and the daily models take sin and cos of the day of year plus a climatology input (`models.py` `season_features`, `batches.py`).
  - **Tail thresholding, but Gaussian-free and not EVT:** gamma upper tail on the gauge's own history (`_gamma_tail`, method of moments), zero-inflated gamma components, and a clean-period percentile threshold set by false-alarm budget (`scale_select.py`, `docs/qc-evaluation-protocol.md`). This is a parametric tail fit to wet days, not a GEV or GPD fit to exceedances.
  - **Decadal tests:** `changepoint.py` (SNHT and Pettitt, simulated critical values, neighbour-difference series). These detect steps, not trends, and hits are labelled "network or sensor change, not distinguishable".
  - **Genuine extremes versus faults:** `extremes.py` (`find_extremes`: protected = a heavy cell-day inside a connected same-day group of at least 4 cells; ambiguous = heavy with no heavy neighbour) and the coherent-extreme discount in `crossscale.py`.
  - **Legacy, not to extend:** `ml-service/training/multiscale.py` (`ScaleNet`: causal trailing means at several window lengths, a seasonal-plus-low-frequency "coarse" stream, attention fusion) and `ml-service/training/model.py` (`LSTMAutoencoder`). These are the old temperature/pressure/humidity-era code scheduled for removal (AGENTS.md section 7); its own docstring marks the multi-scale benefit as an untested hypothesis. It is a causal moving-average decomposition, not wavelets or band-pass filtering.
  - **Hourly:** `backend/app/pipeline/cwc.py` stores raw CWC hourly rows; `backend/app/pipeline/qc.py` rolls them up to rain-day totals (03 UTC to 03 UTC) and runs the daily checks. Nothing models hourly or diurnal structure; hourly peaks are only kept as evidence (`max_hour_mm`).
  - **Autoencoders:** only the legacy LSTM autoencoder above. The live trained model is STNet (`stnet.py`, `stnet_arch.py`), a supervised-on-synthetic-faults network, plus the label-fusion logistic model in `backend/app/pipeline/fusion.py`.

## 5. How each step would fit the standing rules

- **Time-forward splits (AGENTS.md section 10).** Filters must be causal or fitted so that no future sample enters training: a zero-phase wavelet or Lanczos filter leaks future values into the value at time t, and edge effects bite hardest at the most recent end, which is the validation and test end. Plan: fit band definitions, trend and per-band climatology on 1951-2012 only (as `fit_scale_stats` does); apply them to later years with a causal or one-sided filter, or decompose each year inside its own window and report the edge handling. The leakage audit (`splits.py`) would need a rule for filtered series. Whether a non-causal filter is acceptable for offline analysis is an owner decision (open question 3).
- **Locked test years 2018-2025.** No band design choice, filter width, EVT threshold or per-band budget may be picked on those years. Band-model selection goes through the existing validation-only harness; the test years are scored once, for the chosen architecture, per `docs/qc-evaluation-protocol.md`. Adding a per-band scorer family before the existing architecture choice is committed would change the set of candidates after the rule was frozen, so it should wait for the choice or be a labelled follow-up experiment.
- **False alarms per 1,000 clean items as the main cost (section 9).** Running detection in several bands multiplies the chances to fire. The per-band flags must be budgeted jointly (the fused score keeps the existing 1.0 and 5.0 per 1,000 clean cell-days), not each band at its own budget. Report precision, recall, F1, false-alarm rate, detection delay, latency and PR area per band and fused. Accuracy alone is not reported.
- **Genuine extremes never called faults on magnitude alone.** A heavy event is by definition large in the high-frequency band and a coherent heavy-rain system is also large in the intraseasonal band. Band detection must reuse the protected-extreme rule (`extremes.py`) and the coherent-extreme discount, and a flag may rest on magnitude only as "unusual", never as "suspect data" (the repo keeps those two meanings apart, spec section 6). EVT thresholds in particular flag the most extreme genuine values by design, so an EVT tail flag alone must stay an "unusual weather" label.
- **No fakes (section 4).** Every number in a future result is labelled synthetic or real; faults here are injected (synthetic) until real labels exist.

## 6. Risks

1. **Hourly CWC data lists only non-zero hours** (`cwc.py` `ASSUMPTIONS["zero_hours"]`). A missing hour is not known to be dry. A diurnal cycle fitted to this would describe the rain hours only, and any band filter needs a regular series, so filling absent hours with zero is an assumption that must be written down, not a default.
2. **CWC time zone is assumed IST**, not stated by the source; a diurnal cycle is shifted by any error here.
3. **Trend removal with short records.** Hourly and station records of a few years cannot separate a trend from interannual variability; RF25 is long but its gauge network changed over time, which `changepoint.py` already treats as indistinguishable from a real change. Detrending could remove a real regional change or bake a network change into the baseline.
4. **EVT with few extremes.** GEV and GPD fits need enough exceedances, a threshold chosen by stability checks, and stationarity. A single station with a few years has very few; fits would be unstable and the tail probability would be less reliable than the present gamma on 30 or more wet days. Pooling cells or stations would need a regionalisation argument. Not verified in a source this session.
5. **Filter edge effects and leakage** at the end of a series (section 5).
6. **The owner's example events are temperature events, not rainfall.** The 2003 European heatwave, the 2010 Russian heatwave and the 2021 Pacific North-West heat dome (`not verified` here; named by the owner) say nothing about rainfall data validity. They are not rainfall tests and must not be cited as evidence for this system. Rainfall validation events must be chosen and cited separately, for example Indian extreme rainfall events from `docs/research/india-qc-label-and-extreme-event-sources.md` (the Kerala August 2018 case is already listed as unverified in TODO.md, and 2018 sits inside the locked test years, so a 2018 event cannot be used to tune anything).
7. **Per-band multiplication of false alarms** and of tuning freedom (band edges, filter width, thresholds per band): many knobs against few validation years (5) risks overfitting the validation set.
8. **Unsupervised per-band models** (autoencoder, Isolation Forest) are trained on presumed-clean data that may contain real errors, a known failure mode of reconstruction autoencoders (`docs/research/slr-weather-anomaly-detection-models.md`, `read`). The repo's current approach is supervised on injected synthetic faults with the sim-to-real gap as the main unknown.
9. **Novelty.** Wavelet or band-pass decomposition, per-band anomaly detection, autoencoders, Isolation Forest and EVT thresholds are not claimed as novel (AGENTS.md section 5). Any novelty claim needs a citation in `docs/research`; none is made here. Prior art for multi-scale or decomposition-based anomaly detection in general is listed in `docs/research/slr-weather-anomaly-detection-models.md` (`read`); no rainfall-QC-specific literature on the band-wise approach has been searched for this file.

## 7. A staged order if the owner approves (PLANNED, nothing started)
