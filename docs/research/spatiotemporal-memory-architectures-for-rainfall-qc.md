## Summary and recommendation

**Build first: Architecture 1, a per-cell recurrent network (LSTM/GRU, later xLSTM or LRU) with long state, fed with multi-scale same-day neighbourhood summaries, trained with a supervised fault-probability head.** Reasons, in order of strength:

1. **It tests the owner's hypothesis directly and cheaply.** The two missing ingredients are long per-cell memory and same-day context larger than the footprint. A 3 x 3 ablation (memory 0 / 30 / 365 days, context none / small / large) separates them. A full ConvLSTM is a bigger build and a longer train (3 to 15 hours, Section 5), and it cannot isolate which ingredient mattered (INFERENCE).
2. **A supervised head removes the score ceiling.** The earlier note found that scores built from `-log P(dry)` cannot flag a zeroed value (threshold 7.12 nats needs P(wet) >= 0.9992). A fault-probability head trained on synthetic labels has no such bound (INFERENCE from that note, EVIDENCE for the numbers there).
3. **Evidence for the ranking is thin, and I say so.** I found no published paper that detects slow drift, scale or false-zero faults in gridded or gauge rainfall with a ConvLSTM, PredRNN-family, xLSTM or Mamba model (negative search result, not proof of absence). Everything below about these models for QC is INFERENCE. The published work is on forecasting (Section 1), video anomaly detection (Section 2) and language/audio memory (Section 3).
4. **The strong-fault gap is the right target.** In `ceiling.md`, fused recall on STRONG cell-days (change >= 5 mm and footprint weight >= 0.5) is 0.678 overall but, per kind: scale 0.289, drift 0.066, zeroed 0.000 (n = 121, 152, 48). Only 6.5% of fault cell-days are strong, and 86.6% have footprint weight < 0.5 (EVIDENCE: `ceiling.md`, `fusion_logistic_s42`). Success should be judged on strong and centre cells and at event level, not on overall cell-day recall.

Ranking: (1) per-cell memory + multi-scale context, supervised; (2) ConvGRU/ConvLSTM U-Net, supervised, joint space-time memory; (3) self-supervised masked-disc ConvLSTM that predicts a hidden disc larger than the footprint from the rest of the field and history. A long-memory backbone swap (xLSTM, LRU, Mamba) is an ablation inside 1 or 2, not a separate architecture, and is the last thing to try.

Honest caveat on the data (INFERENCE from `ceiling.md`): on a dry day a scale or zeroed fault changes nothing and is not labelled, and a small footprint-fringe change (56% of fault cell-days change under 1 mm) is below rain variability. No model can reach full recall; the ceiling is set by detectability.

Takeaways:
- All of these are forecasters. None targets fault detection (EVIDENCE: the abstracts above state forecasting tasks). The memory they keep is tuned for motion of rain systems over minutes to hours. Our "memory" needs to hold a per-cell offset over weeks (INFERENCE).
- ConvLSTM-type state is fixed-size per pixel, so it is a running summary, not a lookup of old days (INFERENCE; see Section 3 for fixed-state limits).
- Cost note: none of these papers' abstracts report GPU time at our scale. My own costing is in Section 5.

## 3. Long-memory mechanisms: what is shown, and where memory failed

- **xLSTM** (Beck, Pöppel, Spanring, Auer, Prudnikova, Kopp, Klambauer, Brandstetter, Hochreiter 2024): exponential gating; sLSTM with scalar memory and memory mixing; mLSTM with matrix memory and covariance update, fully parallelisable; tested on language modelling, "performs favorably" against Transformers and SSMs. EVIDENCE (abstract). https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04517 . No time-series fault task there.
- **xLSTM in time series.** xLSTMAD (Faber, Pietron, Zurek, Corizzo 2025): encoder-decoder xLSTM, forecasting and reconstruction variants, 17 datasets of TSB-AD-M, VUS-PR, claims to beat 23 baselines. EVIDENCE for the claim as made by the authors (abstract; preprint, I found no independent replication). https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22837 . xLSTM-Mixer (Kraus, Divo, Dhami, Kersting, NeurIPS 2025 poster): forecasting claims. EVIDENCE (abstract). https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16928 . Neither is spatial, zero-inflated or drift-focused.
- **LRU** (Orvieto, Smith, Gu, Fernando, Gulcehre, Pascanu, De 2023): a linear, diagonal recurrence that matches deep SSMs on Long Range Arena at similar speed. EVIDENCE (abstract; preprint). https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06349 . Linear recurrences can be run as a parallel scan, which matters if we need 365-day state cheaply (INFERENCE).
- **S4 and Mamba**: see the earlier note (Gu, Goel, Re 2021; Gu & Dao 2023). Mamba's test domains are language, DNA and audio; no zero-inflated data. EVIDENCE there.
- **Clockwork RNN** (Koutnik, Greff, Gomez, Schmidhuber 2014): hidden layer split into modules updating at different rates, so slow modules hold long-term state; preliminary audio generation and TIMIT tasks only. EVIDENCE (abstract). https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.3511 . The multi-rate idea fits "daily noise plus weekly to seasonal offset" (INFERENCE); a cheap equivalent is feeding 7, 30, 90-day running summaries as inputs.
- **Transformer-XL** (Dai, Yang, Yang, Carbonell, Le, Salakhutdinov, ACL 2019): segment-level recurrence, dependencies 80% longer than RNNs and 450% longer than vanilla Transformers, in language modelling. EVIDENCE (abstract). https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.02860 . Our analogue is truncated back-propagation with state carried across chunks (INFERENCE).
- **Change-point statistics (CUSUM, Page-Hinkley)**: the classical drift tool. I did not open a primary source in this session (Page 1954 not retrieved), so the existence of this method is general knowledge; its performance on this benchmark is UNVERIFIED. The earlier note already proposes it as a cheap baseline.

**Where memory did not help, or is limited (EVIDENCE unless marked):**
- LSTM language models use about 200 tokens of context on average; distant history is a "rough semantic field or topic", and a neural cache helps copying (Khandelwal, He, Qi, Jurafsky, ACL 2018, https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04623). So nominal memory length is not usable memory length.
- Fixed-size-state models (the paper's "generalized state space models") are provably limited at copying from context and do worse than Transformers there (Jelassi, Brandfonbrener, Kakade, Malach 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01032). A ConvLSTM state is also fixed-size, so "recall the exact value from 200 days ago" is not something to expect (INFERENCE). A running offset estimate is a different, easier thing to store.
- The earlier note records: TimeSeAD "no single method dominates", older LSTM-AE competitive; Kim et al. 2022 untrained baselines close to published methods; Zeng et al. 2022 linear models beat Transformer forecasters; several Mamba time-series papers note struggles with non-stationary data. All EVIDENCE as stated there. Gains from a new memory architecture must be shown against seed spread and a no-memory baseline.
- I found no paper showing long memory helps detect slow drift or persistent offsets in gridded rainfall. That claim is INFERENCE and is exactly what the ablation in Section 5 tests.

## 4. Zero-inflated, intermittent targets

- Hunt 2025, "Stop using root-mean-square error as a precipitation target!", arXiv 2509.08369: Tweedie deviance replaces RMSE; for ConvLSTM nowcasting over Kolkata, wet-pixel MAE improved about 2% at t+1 and about 16% at t+4, with better dry-pixel hit rates. EVIDENCE (abstract; dataset details not given there). For 1 < p < 2 the Tweedie is a compound Poisson-Gamma with a point mass at zero (from the paper's description via search record). https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08369
- MZ-Rain: Tweedie amount plus an occurrence auxiliary head (that the occurrence head does not feed the amount is from a search description, UNVERIFIED). https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04864
- DropsToGrid (arXiv 2605.05912) uses a Bernoulli occurrence plus Gamma intensity per grid cell; Deep Extreme Transformer (AAAI) uses a Tweedie head. UNVERIFIED (search snippets only).
- I found no head-to-head of a Tweedie head against an explicit zero-inflated Gamma head (negative search result).
- Fit to us (INFERENCE): we already train a zero-inflated Gamma. For QC the likelihood is an auxiliary job (expected amount and P(wet) given context). The detection output should be a direct fault-probability head, or a signed residual (observed minus expected), not the tail probability of the observed value, because the dry-day tail score is bounded (earlier note). Tweedie is an option for the auxiliary head, not a fix for recall.

## 5. Fit to our setting: three architectures, ranked

Common setup (INFERENCE). Frames: 62 years x 365 = about 22,645 days; full grid 129 x 135 = 17,415 pixels, 4,964 land; 4,964 x 22,645 = 1.12e8 cell-days. Float32 field = 0.45 GB, so it lives on the GPU. Faults are injected on the GPU in each sampled block, with kind, radius (1.5 to 4) and strength sampled from the training pool. Hold out whole fault kinds and radius 4 for testing generalisation. Report at budget 1 false alert per 1,000 clean cell-days on the strong subset, centre cells and event level, with three seeds (n is small: strong zeroed n = 48 gives a 95% interval of roughly +-0.14 at recall 0.5, INFERENCE). All wall-time figures below are reasoned estimates, not benchmarks, and assume AMP.

### Architecture 1 (build first): per-cell recurrent network with multi-scale same-day context, supervised

- **Input per cell-day t:** own log1p(mm) and valid flag; same-day neighbourhood means of log1p(mm) over discs of radius 2, 4, 8 and 16 cells, and the wet fraction at radius 8; own minus pooled value at radius 8 and 16; static climatology of that cell and day of year, sin/cos day of year; optionally slow summaries (7, 30, 90-day running means of own-minus-pool). About 15 channels. Pools are computed from the corrupted field, so a footprint of radius 4 only dilutes the radius 8 and 16 means (INFERENCE).
- **Output:** (a) P(fault) for that cell-day, (b) event-type logits (scale, zeroed, drift, spike, stuck, phantom, none) for explanation, (c) auxiliary expected amount and P(wet) from the clean target (zero-inflated Gamma or Tweedie).
- **Where long memory lives:** the LSTM/GRU hidden state of each cell (2 layers, hidden 128), run over 90-day chunks with state carried across chunks (truncated BPTT, as the Transformer-XL idea) to cover 365 days. Variants: xLSTM mLSTM matrix memory, LRU, or a Clockwork-style multi-rate input (Section 3).
- **How it detects:** zeroed and scale: day-t own-minus-pool at radius 8 to 16 is strongly negative (zero in a wet area) or off-ratio, for any memory length. Drift: the state accumulates a persistent, signed own-minus-pool offset over 21 to 60 days that a single day cannot reveal (INFERENCE, to be tested).
- **Training regime:** supervised with unlimited synthetic labels (focal or weighted BCE for P(fault), CE for kind), plus the clean-target auxiliary. Leave-one-kind-out runs show whether it generalises. Risk: it may learn generator signatures; mitigate with held-out radius and kind, and by checking that auxiliary-head residual alone gives partial recall (INFERENCE).
- **Cost:** about 15 inputs; per cell-day GRU forward is about 6 x 128 x (128 + 128) + 6 x 128 x (15 + 128) = 0.31 MFLOP, times 3 for forward plus backward is about 0.9 MFLOP. Over 1.12e8 cell-days: about 1e14 FLOP per epoch. At 2 to 5 TFLOPS effective for small recurrent kernels that is under a minute of arithmetic; allowing for sequential steps, pooling and fault injection I expect 5 to 15 minutes per full epoch on an A6000-class card. Memory: 16,384 sequences x 90 steps x about 1 KB saved per step is about 1.5 GB for the recurrent part; budget 6 to 12 GB with everything. A useful model should need fewer than one epoch of random crops (e.g. 96 x 96 cells x 120 days). cuDNN handles LSTM/GRU; xLSTM or Mamba kernels on Windows are UNVERIFIED (earlier note found Mamba lacks documented Windows support), but a pure-PyTorch loop over 90 steps is acceptable.
- **Falsifiable prediction (strong subset, centre cells and strong cells; current recall 0.29 scale, 0.07 drift, 0.00 zeroed):** with large context and 365-day memory, strong scale >= 0.60, strong zeroed >= 0.60, strong drift >= 0.30 at budget 1. FAIL if strong scale or zeroed < 0.40 with large context (the information is not in the data at this false-alarm budget), or if drift < 0.15. Ablations that decide the memory question: (i) large context, memory 0 days must match the full model on scale and zeroed (if not, context alone is not enough) and must be clearly worse on drift; if it matches on drift, long memory is NOT needed and the owner's premise is falsified for this benchmark; (ii) full memory with no context must stay below 0.15 on drift (own history alone cannot separate drift from weather); (iii) held-out kind recall >= 50% of seen-kind recall, and held-out radius 4 loses <= 0.10 recall.

### Architecture 2: ConvGRU/ConvLSTM U-Net, supervised, memory in a spatial state

- **Input:** a sequence of full-field days (log1p mm, valid mask, climatology, day-of-year, lat/lon) for K = 90 days, with state carried across chunks for longer horizons. Encoder strides to about 65 x 68 and 33 x 34, ConvLSTM or ConvGRU layers at both scales (receptive field above 12 cells), decoder back to full resolution. Mask ocean.
- **Output:** per-cell, per-day fault probability and kind logits, plus clean-field expected amount and P(wet).
- **Where long memory lives:** the convolutional cell state at two scales; it can hold spatially coherent offsets (a footprint patch that stays low relative to its surroundings), which a per-cell memory cannot see jointly (INFERENCE). Swap the cell for PredRNN++ Causal LSTM or MIM if the plain cell underfits (EVIDENCE that those exist for forecasting; no QC evidence).
- **How it detects:** scale and zeroed from the patch against the surrounding field at the larger scale on the same day; drift from the state at the patch location accumulating the offset over weeks.
- **Training:** supervised with synthetic labels as for Architecture 1; same generalisation tests. Needs patch-level augmentation to avoid memorising grid position (INFERENCE).
- **Cost:** per frame, two ConvLSTM layers at 4,420 positions (3x3, 48 channels): 4 x 48 x 96 x 9 = 166k MAC, so about 1.5 GFLOP per layer; one layer at 1,122 positions (64 channels): about 0.7 GFLOP; encoder and decoder about 1 GFLOP; about 5 GFLOP forward and 15 GFLOP with backward. One pass over 22,645 frames is about 3.4e14 FLOP, which is 1 to 5 minutes of arithmetic at 1 to 5 TFLOPS effective, but ConvLSTM has no fused kernel, so I expect 3 to 10 minutes per frame-pass. Saved activations are about 20 MB per frame under AMP, so a batch of 8 sequences x 90 days (720 frames) is about 14 GB, which fits in 48 GB. Only about 31 gradient steps per frame-pass at that batch size, so about 3,000 steps is roughly 95 passes: about 5 to 15 hours. Spatial crops of 96 x 96 shorten this.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** at least as good as Architecture 1 on strong scale and zeroed (>= 0.60 each) and clearly better on drift (>= 0.45) where the footprint patch persists. FAIL: no gain of at least 0.05 absolute over Architecture 1 on strong drift, which means joint spatial memory adds nothing and the extra cost is not justified.

### Architecture 3: self-supervised masked-disc ConvLSTM, trained on clean data

- **Input:** the K-day field history plus the day-t field with a disc of radius R (R of 5 or more, larger than the maximum footprint of 4) hidden; the net must predict the hidden disc.
- **Output:** distribution (zero-inflated Gamma or Tweedie) for each hidden cell. Score: signed residual of the observed value against the predicted distribution (expected amount, P(wet)), over discs placed on a stride-4 lattice so every cell is covered about five times (INFERENCE: the area pi x 25 over 16 gives about 5).
- **Where long memory lives:** the ConvLSTM state of the history; slow offsets show up as a persistent signed residual over days, which a CUSUM-style accumulator on the residual can integrate.
- **Training:** self-supervised on clean years, so no kind labels and generalisation to unseen kinds by construction. Real RF25 contains real errors, treated as noise (INFERENCE). Related ideas: future-frame prediction error for anomaly detection (Liu et al. 2018, Section 2) and the memory-of-normal idea (MemAE).
- **Cost:** about Architecture 2 plus about 5x at inference for the disc lattice; training about the same 5 to 15 hours.
- **Weaknesses:** the hidden disc removes exactly the pixels that carry the fault but a footprint straddling the disc boundary leaks; and the earlier note's bound applies: a dry observation scored by tail probability cannot flag a zero, so use the signed expected-amount residual (INFERENCE).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** strong scale >= 0.45, strong zeroed >= 0.45, strong drift >= 0.2 (the weakest of the three because there is no label to tell drift from weather). FAIL: strong zeroed < 0.20 with R at 5 or more, meaning the self-supervised residual is too noisy at this budget; then rely on supervised Architectures 1 and 2.

### Why this order

- 1 is cheapest, uses cuDNN, has the cleanest ablation, and gives the first evidence on whether long memory matters.
- 2 is the full spatiotemporal model the owner asks for; justified only if 1 shows both ingredients matter and a joint state could add more.
- 3 is the hedge for unseen fault kinds; run it if 1 and 2 fail the held-out-kind test.
- xLSTM, LRU or Mamba backbones: try only after the GRU/LSTM ablation shows long memory helps and that the plain LSTM saturates. The published case for them is for much longer sequences (Section 3) than our 90 to 365 steps (INFERENCE).

## What this document does not settle

- No result here shows any of these architectures detects drift, scale or false-zero faults in gridded rainfall; there is no direct published evidence either way, and all predictions are hypotheses.
- Costs are reasoned from FLOP counts, not measured. Batch size, data-loading and kernel efficiency could change them by a factor of several.
- UNVERIFIED: MetNet's ConvLSTM encoder, MIM venue, Mamba-based nowcasting papers (MambaRain, TransMambaCNN, VMU-Diff), the Wu et al. PM2.5 GNN paper, Mallet et al. 2022, the 2026 EGUsphere preprint, the hybrid CNN-LSTM autoencoder (buoy and Katrina), PRNet, LE3D, DropsToGrid, Deep Extreme Transformer, CUSUM source, xLSTM and Mamba kernel support on Windows, and all numbers in `comparison_s42.json` and `ablation_s42.json` (taken from the earlier note).
- The strong subsets are small (n of 48 to 152 per kind in validation); increase validation events before drawing conclusions.
